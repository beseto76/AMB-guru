## Summary
DBC is a ceramic substrate bonding technology that bonds copper foil directly to a ceramic base (e.g. Al2O3, AlN, ZrO2-doped HPS) by oxidizing the copper surface, without an intermediate active metal layer.

## Details
- Bonding relies on oxidation of copper foils, which lowers the melt temperature from 1,083°C to 1,065°C (per [[advanced-packaging-designs-keystone-project-1-publications-nlr]]).
- Maximum metallization thickness of 1 mm; requires metallization layers on both sides of the ceramic.
- Used with aluminum oxide (Al2O3), aluminum nitride (AlN), and zirconia (ZrO2)-doped high-performance substrates (HPS).
- Generally simpler and cheaper than AMB, but AMB is preferred where the ceramic (e.g. Si3N4) doesn't bond well via direct oxidation.
- In the combined DCB+AMB power-module market, DCB holds the larger share (62.4% per Dataintelo) due to lower cost and mature manufacturing infrastructure, but grows more slowly (6.8% CAGR) than AMB (10.2% CAGR) as SiC devices push demand toward AMB/Si3N4 ([[dcb-and-amb-substrates-for-power-modules-market-research-report-2034]]).
- DCB/AlN offers an intermediate performance tier (~170 W/m·K thermal conductivity) between DCB/Al2O3 and AMB/Si3N4, but its lower fracture toughness (~3.5 MPa·m^0.5) limits use in the most demanding automotive thermal-cycling profiles.
- Sits within a much larger "ceramic substrate" category (Alumina, AlN, BeO, Si3N4 spanning consumer electronics and telecom, not just power modules) sized at USD 8.7B in 2025 — a broader, non-power-module-specific market context ([[ceramic-substrate-market-research-report-2033]]).
