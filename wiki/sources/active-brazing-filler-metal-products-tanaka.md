## Summary
TANAKA Precious Metal Group's own product page for its active brazing filler metals — the Ag-Cu-Ti and Ag-Cu-Sn-Ti alloys used to braze copper directly onto ceramics (including non-oxide ceramics like Si3N4) without prior metallization. A primary-source (vendor) technical reference for the material actually used inside AMB substrates, complementing the market-research reports elsewhere in this wiki.

## Key Claims
- Active brazing filler metal bonds ceramics (oxides or nitrides) and carbon materials to metal without metallization, under high-temperature vacuum brazing.
- Flagship product TKC-661 (AgCuTi alloy, 66% Ag / 29.5% Cu / 1.5% Ti): solidus 745°C, liquidus 780°C, hardness 113 HV, tensile strength 356 MPa, thermal conductivity 102.0 W/m·K.
- Compared to a conventional (metallized) brazing alloy BAg-8: TKC-661 achieves ceramic bonding directly (BAg-8 cannot: "Ceramic bonding: ✓ vs. ✗"), at the cost of lower thermal conductivity (102 vs. 311 W/m·K) but higher tensile strength (356 vs. 294 MPa).
- Four-point bend testing: bonds made with BAg-8 (metallized) fractured at the bond interface; bonds made with active brazing filler metal fractured in the base material instead — i.e., the active-braze bond is stronger than the ceramic itself.
- SEM/EDX analysis shows a titanium layer forms at both the ceramic and filler interfaces, with an Al-Ti-O compound layer forming between the Ti layer and alumina.
- Also offers an "Active Brazing Filler Metal/Copper Composite Material" — active braze pre-clad onto one side of copper — intended for next-generation power-device heat sinks and ceramic substrates, enabling higher thermal conductivity with fewer processing steps. Sample: TKC-661 (0.02mm) / Cu (0.8mm) / Si3N4 (0.32mm) / Cu (0.8mm) heat-cycle test structure.
